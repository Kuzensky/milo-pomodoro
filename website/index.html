    The idea

    Most timers just count down. Milo keeps you company. A small cat in the corner of your screen, quietly paying attention while you do the thing you said you’d do. When time is up, Milo lets you know. Then it stretches. You should too.

    Fair questions

    Before you ask.

    01macOS says it can’t verify Milo. Now what?

    That’s expected. Milo isn’t notarized by Apple yet, so macOS asks before opening it the first time.

    1. Open Milo.dmg and drag Milo into Applications.
    2. Open Milo. When macOS says it can’t verify the app, click Done.
    3. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway.

    You only do this once. Comfortable in Terminal? xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Milo.app does the same. The source is on GitHub if you’d rather build it yourself.

    02Which Macs does Milo run on?

    Any Mac with macOS 26 or later. Milo is a universal app, built for both Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

    03Does Milo collect any data?

    No. There’s no account, no server and no analytics. Your sessions and settings never leave your Mac.

    04What if my Mac sleeps mid-session?

    Nothing is lost. Milo remembers when the session ends rather than counting ticks, so the time is right when you come back. A session that finished while you were away counts once.

    05Can Milo sit still?

    Yes. Turn off Animate Milo in Settings. Milo also stays still when Reduce Motion is on, and rests whenever the panel is closed.

    06How do I quit or uninstall?

    Right-click the cat in the menu bar and choose Quit. To uninstall, quit Milo and drag it from Applications to the Trash.
